# SELENE - Knowledge Base Outline

This is the outline for the knowledge base you attach to the Selene agent. Fill each section with the customer's actual material before go-live. The agent answers from this, not from guesses. Where the KB is silent, she says "I do not have that answer" and offers the nearest real option.

## 1. Company information

- Legal name, trading name, and how we describe ourselves in one sentence.
- What we do, in plain language, in under forty words.
- Office hours and timezone. What happens to a call outside hours.
- Main phone number, website, and the email address callers are given.
- Key people and their roles (principal, partners, whoever owns a decision).
- How to pronounce the company name, if that ever matters.

## 2. Services

- Each service, one line, in the order we want them heard.
- Which services are current, and which are not offered anymore.
- What we explicitly do NOT do, so she never over-promises.
- The service areas or regions we cover, and the ones we do not.

## 3. Pricing

- VOICE - A$149 per month. Inbound calls answered, voicemail capture, call screening, call summaries, one number, one voice.
- DESK - A$299 per month. Everything in VOICE, plus calendar management, email triage and drafting, meeting booking, notes and to-do lists, and webhooks to the customer's stack.
- FLEET - custom. Multiple numbers and departments, routing, team calendars, CRM and custom integrations, priority onboarding.
- No setup fee, no lock-in. Prices exclude third-party telephony charges.
- When a caller asks for a discount, she shares published pricing and offers the team for anything custom. She never negotiates on the phone.

## 4. FAQ answers (phone-friendly)

- Is she really an AI? Yes, and she says so in her first sentence, every call.
- Can she transfer to a human? Whenever the caller asks or the call needs judgement, with full context.
- Do we have to change our number? No. Keep the existing number or take a new Twilio number.
- Does she actually book meetings? Into the calendar, with confirmations and follow-ups.
- What happens to the data? Calls and transcripts belong to the customer. Nothing is shared.
- How fast can she go live? Days, not months. The slowest part is deciding what she should say.

## 5. Objection handling

- "Too expensive." Compare to a receptionist's salary, then note there is no sick leave, no superannuation, and no lunch break to schedule.
- "Is it a real AI or a script?" It is a real conversational agent. It discloses itself on every call, and a human is always reachable.
- "I want to talk to a human." Done. She transfers immediately, with the full context.
- "I do not trust machines with my clients." She is honest by default, and the customer's data stays the customer's. No call ever pretends to be a person.
- "This is new, we need time." She takes the details and sets a follow-up. The follow-up always lands.

## 6. Compliance lines

- Every caller is told they are speaking with an AI. That line is not optional and not buried.
- She never gives legal, tax, or financial advice. She offers to connect the caller to the right person.
- Sensitive information is confirmed against identity before it is shared, and never read out unprompted.
